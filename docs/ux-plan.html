UI, audio and controls plan

I captured all 14 screens at six sizes (small phone 360×640, phone 390×844, phone sideways, tablet upright and sideways, desktop) in a headless browser and measured scrolling, overlaps and text size on each. This is what is wrong, what I propose, and the order I would do it in. I have not tested on a real device.

1. What the audit found

Hideout (shop)

  • You cannot see an upgrade and its details together on a phone. The details and the Buy button sit below the list, off screen, on all six tabs. Every purchase needs a scroll.
  • The top of the screen is spent on chrome. Title, coins and two rows of tabs take 330 of 844 px on a phone. On a small phone the list window is 260 px, about four rows.
  • Ship stats are only on two of six tabs (Ship upgrades, Ships), shown as marks with no numbers, and with no preview of what a purchase changes. Weapons show no damage or fire-rate figures at all.
  • Overlap: the level marks on Ship upgrades run into the upgrade name at every screen size.

In flight

  • The playfield uses a little over half the phone's height. There are empty bands above and below the cloth.
  • Text is too small: HUD labels are 9 px, combo 10 px, power-up names 10.5 px. Twelve HUD elements are under 12 px.
  • Keyboard hints on a touch screen: the item slots show "1 2 3 Esc" keycaps.
  • Pause sits in the same row as the item slots, one thumb-slip from using an item.
  • Power-ups are text boxes that wrap onto a second row; the bonus-wave caption is cut off with "…".
  • Pop-up notices land on other things: "Blueprint found" covers the stage-clear heading, the Resume button on a sideways phone, and the tabs on Records.

Other screens

  • How to play lists keyboard keys first on a phone, and its Back button is cut off at 390×844.
  • Records on a phone squeezes the table to 188 px next to the stats, so it scrolls sideways and down (1.9 screens).
  • Sideways phone in a browser is broken: the hideout list is 49 px tall and the playfield is stamp-sized. The Android app is locked upright, so only the web build is affected.
  • Tablet upright and desktop are mostly fine: one or two lists scroll by a row, and labels are 11 px.

Audio

  • 14 sound effects cover about 25 game events. All 8 weapons share one firing sound; one "hurt" sound covers four different events.
  • No sound for menu taps, tab changes, a purchase you can't afford, dash, weapon swap, combo milestones or low shield.
  • Music cuts hard between tracks at one fixed volume. There is a single Sound on/off switch and no vibration.

Controls

  • One finger sets the ship's left-right position; firing is automatic. The ship never leaves the bottom line.
  • Your thumb sits on the ship, and touches only register on the cloth, not the space below it.
  • Dash and weapon swap exist, but each only on one ship, and dash is a double-tap.

2. Hideout redesign

  • One-line header with coins and gems; one row of six tabs. Frees about 190 px on a phone.
  • "Your ship" strip that never leaves the screen: damage, fire rate, shield and speed as real numbers, on every tab. Selecting anything shows the change in green before you buy (an upgrade, a different ship, a weapon's damage and rate).
  • Details dock to the bottom, always visible with the Buy button. Tap a row, read, buy: no scrolling.
  • Tighter rows (about 52 px, still above the 48 px touch minimum) so six to eight fit without scrolling on a 390×844 phone.
  • Collection becomes a grid of picture tiles; tap one for its note. Roughly five screens down to one or two.
  • Tablet upright gets list and details side by side; desktop keeps its layout and gains the stat strip.

3. Flight screen redesign

  • Playfield fills the width and at least 70% of the height on a phone; one slim status row on top.
  • Power-ups become icon rings that drain as they run out, stacked on the left edge. No wrapping text row.
  • Item buttons move to the right edge (switchable for left-handers); Pause moves to the top corner, away from items. No keycaps on touch screens.
  • No text under 12 px anywhere.
  • Notices get their own lane under the status row, so they never cover a heading or button.

Remaining screens

  • How to play shows touch controls on touch devices, fits one screen, and becomes a short playable first-run tutorial once the new controls exist.
  • Records stacks on phones: runs as rows, stats behind a second tab. No sideways scrolling.
  • One Settings sheet (music and effects volume, vibration, control style, handedness, shadows) replaces the loose toggles on the menu and pause screens.
  • Sideways phones in a browser get the compact side-panel layout the desktop uses.

4. New ways to play

What good touch shooters do, briefly: CAVE's phone ports and Sky Force use relative drag (the ship moves as far as your thumb moves, from wherever you touch), which stops the thumb hiding the ship. Sky Force slows the game when you lift your finger. Aka to Blue fires a bullet-clearing bomb on a second-finger tap. None of them add a joystick or a row of buttons.

5. Audio and visual upgrades

Audio

  • A distinct firing sound for each of the 8 weapons, and separate sounds where one is reused today (shield hit, life lost, capture).
  • New sounds: menu tap, tab change, can't-afford, dash, swap, special, near-miss, combo milestones that rise in pitch, a low-shield warning, a boss-arrival sting.
  • Music crossfades between tracks, dips under big sounds, muffles on pause, and gains an intensity layer at high combo.
  • Separate music and effects volume. Vibration on hits, dash, purchases and boss kills.
  • Cost: about 22 new ElevenLabs sounds and one music layer, on your credits.

Visual

  • Stat numbers count up and a thread stitches along the bar when you buy; coins fly to the price.
  • Stitched wipe between screens instead of a hard swap.
  • Boss intro card with name and weak spot; screen edge frays red at one shield.
  • Combo shown as a thread winding around the score; dash leaves a stitched trail; the special visibly unravels bullets.

7. Two weapons, always firing: lead and bobbin

The idea: your lead weapon fires from the ship. Your second weapon fires from a bobbin, a small spool tied to the ship by a thread, that trails behind your movement. One finger steers both.

  • Hold still and the bobbin tucks in under the ship: both weapons pour into one column. That is your boss-damage stance.
  • Sweep sideways and the bobbin swings out behind you: two firing points cover a wide stretch of the cloth.
  • Flick-dash and the bobbin whips across after you. The thread between ship and bobbin cuts anything it sweeps through.
  • Cross-stitch: an enemy hit by both weapons within one second takes 25% more damage and fills the special meter faster. Lining the two up on one target is the skill.

Any weapon you own can go in either slot. The bobbin weapon fires at 60% damage so two weapons do not double your power. The second slot opens as soon as you own a second weapon. No swap button, no extra input.

Some weapons behave differently on the bobbin: the Wire Laser charges only while the bobbin is at rest, the Knot Bomb drops from wherever the bobbin is, the Cross Stitch bounces from the bobbin's side of the cloth.

Why this and not the alternatives: firing the second weapon on a timer adds nothing to do; a mix that shifts with how far forward you fly hides the second weapon half the time; a second-thumb fire button is the clutter we ruled out. A trailing second firing point turns plain left-right movement into aiming, and it is a thread, which suits the game.

8. Weapon retune

Measured from the code at level III, in the same damage units: Spread Stitch does 11.7 per second with the widest coverage and is the second-cheapest weapon (600 coins). Cross is 10.7, Satin 9.9, Double 9.2, Chain up to 7.1, Wire Laser 5.3, Single 4.3. Spread wins on power, coverage and price at once, which is why it is always the pick.

The fix is to give each weapon one job and a matching weakness, and to add enemies that punish the wrong tool.

  • Enemies that ask for a tool: armoured (heavy hits or piercing), swarms (chain, spread), front-shielded (cross, bomb). The route card names which kind a route is full of, so you pick a pair of weapons for the road ahead.
  • Prices follow power. Target: at equal level, every weapon lands within 15% of the others across a mixed run, measured by bot runs with each weapon in each slot.
  • Weapon stats in the shop become real numbers (shots, damage per shot, per second) once the weapon table is data rather than code.

9. Nine ships, nine ways to fly

Every ship gets a passive trait, its own special (second-finger tap, charged by near-misses) and usually a twist on the bobbin. Chenille's "carries two weapons" and Tapestry's "drone" are replaced, because every ship now has two weapons and a bobbin.
