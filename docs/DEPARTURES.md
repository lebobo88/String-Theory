# Departures from the Mendnight art bible

Each entry: what changed, why, and how it came about.

## Phase 1 layout pass (November 2026)

- **Hideout is one screen, not a list above a detail panel.** Header shrunk to one line, six tabs in one row, a stat strip that is always visible, a two-column list, and the details docked at the bottom. Why: on a 390x844 phone the bible's layout put the details and the Buy button below the fold on every tab (1.4 to 5.1 screens of scrolling), and ship stats were visible on only two tabs. Found by measuring every screen at six sizes.
- **Stats are numbers with a before/after preview** instead of rows of stitched marks. Why: marks could not show what a purchase changes, and on the Upgrades list they ran into the upgrade name at every screen size. The marks remain inside the upgrade's detail panel.
- **Collection is a grid of picture tiles** with one detail panel, instead of a list of entry rows. Why: the list was 5 screens long on a phone and 9 on a small phone.
- **Phone flight HUD is a slim status row** with icon-only lives and shield, and Pause moved into its corner. Why: labels were 9 px, and Pause sat next to the item slots.
- **The phone camera zooms until the cloth spans the screen**, so the frame's side rails run off the edges. Why: the cloth was using roughly half the screen height.
- **No text under 12 px anywhere.** The bible's 11 px eyebrow labels are now 12 px.
- **Notices never sit on a sheet.** They are hidden while any menu is open (the same facts appear on the result screens) and moved off the playfield in flight.
- **Loose setting switches became one Settings sheet.**

## Phase 2 controls and play (November 2026)

- **The cloth is taller and the ship roams it.** The field gained 3.4 units at the bottom and the ship can fly 5.6 units up from its home row. Why: on a phone the cloth was bound by its width and filled 58% of the screen height; the owner asked for more to do than sliding left and right.
- **Every ship carries two weapons that both fire all the time.** The lead weapon fires from the ship, the second from a bobbin that trails on a thread. The bible gave two weapons to the Chenille only, behind a swap button. Why: the owner asked for both weapons always on with no swapping. The thread to the bobbin cuts enemies after a dash.
- **Ship traits were rewritten** so each ship has its own special (charged by near-misses, fired with a second finger) and a trait that is not just a stat. Chenille's "two weapons" and Tapestry's "drone" became a cutting tether and twin bobbins, since every ship now has two weapons and a bobbin. The Bobbin Drone item and power-up are unchanged.
- **Weapons were retuned around one job each.** At level III the Spread Stitch out-damaged everything (11.7 a second against 4.3 to 10.7) with the widest coverage at the second-lowest price. It now fades with distance. Numbers live in `THREADS[id].lv` and `tools/bench.js` measures them.
- **Dash is on every ship** (flick), time slows when you lift your finger, and steering is relative drag. The bible's steering put the ship under the thumb.
- **Stage results list what you picked up, then each bonus, then the total.** Before, "earned" already included the bonuses listed under it, so the lines did not add up.

## Boss rebuild and performance (November 2026)

- **Every boss is a script of its own** (`src/boss/*.js` on the framework in `src/bosses.js`), replacing the shared table of timed attacks. Why: all eight swayed along the top edge on one sine wave, drew from one list of eleven attacks, and were beaten the same way. Each now has its own movement, one mechanic nobody else has, and its own way to be hurt. Full design: `docs/boss-plan.html`.
- **Bosses have a fray meter and can be stunned.** Weak-spot damage fills it; a stunned boss takes half again as much.
- **Bosses put things on the cloth** (egg sacs, fuses, web lines, charge lanes and so on) and three of them change the arena itself: lights out, rows unravelled from the bottom, edges fraying in.
- **Boss sizes changed** where the fight needed it: the Carpet Beetle and the Silk Empress are smaller so they can move through the ship's half of the cloth.
- **Each boss has an entrance film** (`src/cine.js`): the first camera movement in the game. Four seconds, six for the Moth King, 1.5 once seen, a tap skips.
- **Phones start on a new "lite" graphics tier** (glow, no shadow pass, no lens passes), the HUD updates ten times a second instead of every frame, drawing is capped to one frame per game step, and the Android app asks the screen for 60Hz. Why: the HUD was being rebuilt on 54 of 55 frames after two always-on weapons made events constant, and 90Hz phones were drawing every frame twice in three.

## Finishing pass (November 2026)

- **Boss strength follows where the player is in the run** (`POWER` in `src/bosses.js`). The eight boss scripts were tuned against a fully built ship, which made the first boss a slog for a new player. Each boss's health and the health of what it puts on the cloth are now scaled to the ship a player has by that panel. Tuned with the bot in `tools/bossbot.js`, which flies a typical loadout for each panel; it has not been tuned by hand play.
- **A new enemy, the shield bug**, appears from panel 5. It stops straight shots while in formation; curved, bouncing and bobbin shots get past, and its guard drops for a moment after each block. Why: the retuned weapons each have one job, and nothing in the enemy set rewarded picking by job.
- **Each route card names what waits on it**, so the weapon choice can be made before flying.
- **Enemy rows fire 10% more often** now that the ship can dodge up and down, dash and slow time.
- **Sound.** 67 new effects and two more boss music tracks (panels 4 to 6, and the Moth King). Every weapon, every ship special, every boss mechanic and every entrance film has its own sound. Music crossfades and dips under big moments. Music and effects have their own volume sliders.
- **Vibration** on hits, specials and boss moments, with a switch in Settings.
- **The first flight teaches by doing**: one prompt at a time (steer, lift to slow, flick to dash, special), each cleared by doing the thing. It replaces the text notices.
- **Interface.** Purchases show what changed, screens change with a stitched wipe, power-ups are small stitched badges, the combo is thread wound round the score. Phones held sideways and left-handed play each have their own layout. On the shortest phones the route map lays cleared and upcoming stages two to a row so it never scrolls, and the camera sits closer so the cloth fills the height.
- **Drawing.** Each ship's special has its own effect in place of one shared ring. Boss light cones stop at the edge of the cloth. A last point of shield frays the screen edges red.

## Power-up upgrades

- **Every power-up has one permanent upgraded form, bought with gems** (`C.CHARMS[id].up`, saved as `profile.charmUps`). Once bought, that power-up always drops and plays as its upgraded form. New: neither the bible nor DESIGN.md had a second thing to spend gems on beside ships and perks. Asked for by the owner ("the twin ship could turn into a triple ship").
- **Three rows, cheap first.** Row 1 (25 to 45 gems) is open from the start; row 2 (70 to 100) opens after any two from row 1; row 3 (150 to 220) after any two from row 2. 1020 gems for all eleven. Why these prices: the sim pays about 10 to 15 gems for a run that ends at the first or second boss and 157 to 177 for a full Normal run (Hard x1.5, Nightmare x2.2), so the first upgrade comes in the first run or two, the Triple Ship (with the four it needs) at about 425 gems, and the whole set is a goal on the scale of the ships (1280).
- **They live on the Perks tab, as its second view**, with a Perks / Power-ups switch in the row the stats strip uses on other tabs. Why not a seventh tab: seven tabs did not fit one row on a 360 px phone (the six already fill it), nor the side column of a sideways phone, and the owner groups perks with power-ups. The stats strip is not needed there: perks that move a ship number now show it before and after in their details, and power-up upgrades show the power-up now and after.
- **Upgraded power-ups are embroidered**: their badge in flight is the same stitched picture ringed with saffron stitches in a doubled gold frame, the pickup gains a gold hoop and three gold stitches circling it, and picking one up throws a second, gold loop. The Triple Ship flies in a V (the two extra ships a little behind), and the drones move out past its wings.
- **Seeker Shots chase enemies, not the boss.** A first version also homed on the boss's weak spot; it made three fights slower (the Great Wick from 77 to 286 seconds, the Silk Empress from 115 to 223) because those weak spots open through things on the cloth the shots were being pulled away from. Measured with `tools/bossbot.js`.
- **Twin Drones' shots curve toward enemies.** Two plain drones cleared waves only 5% faster than one; with curving shots it is 20%.
- **The wave tally with its quick shop fits one screen**: two columns on wide screens, tighter rows and the item names only on short phones. Found while auditing this work: with two blueprints and the quick shop it ran past a 1280x720 window and a 360x640 phone.
- **Past eight running power-ups, the badges on wide screens drop their names** (picture only, as on phones), so the item slots and Pause stay on screen.

## Second finishing pass (November 2026)

- **The canvas runs the full height of a phone screen, behind the status row and the item row.** It used to stop where the item row begins, so fireworks and boss effects ended on a straight line there. The cloth is fitted to the open space between the two rows and the view is shifted to centre it (`resize()` in `src/render.js`, the `.clothbox` probe in `style.css`).
- **A phone's High graphics keeps every effect but uses 2x smoothing and a 1024 shadow map**, and when a tier has been picked by hand only sharpness slides when frames run long (auto still steps tiers). Why: the owner set High on a Pixel 7 and saw the frame rate fall from the second stage on. The game's own work per frame is flat across all eight stages; the cost that grows is on the graphics chip, with about 40 enemies on screen from stage 2 against 24 in stage 1.
- **Spark and debris pools draw only what is alive** instead of every slot every frame.
- **Effects play from decoded buffers where the page is served** (the phone app), and from audio elements only when opened from disk.
- **Every boss has a death film** (`DEATHS` in `src/cine.js`) with its own sound, and the shortened entrance for a boss already seen is its own 1.5 second beat (`SIGS`) with its own sting, in place of a shared tail and one shared sound. With "remove animations" on, each entrance holds one frame of that boss's own film; before, all eight showed the same title over an empty cloth.
- **What a boss drops appears when its death film ends**, not under it.

## Polish pass: effects (A1)

- **Nothing an effect draws ends on a straight line.** The table, the cloth and the frame are now drawn first and the depth buffer is cleared before anything else, so sprites, rings, trails and bursts are drawn over them whatever their height. Why: the "line under the fireworks" was the cloth itself. Effect sprites face the lens and the cloth leans away from it by 0.2 rad, so any large ring or flare low over the cloth was sliced along a level line below its centre, and anything flat was hidden under the raised frame rails. Found by capturing a perfect bonus wave at 412x915 and looking at one hoop at full size.
- **Large rings are flat hoops of thread (strips), not point sprites.** Point sprites were also capped at 300 pixels, so the big rings of a knot burst or a boss's death never reached their size. They are thin on purpose now that they do.
- **Ripples in the cloth die away over the last stitches before the frame**, light cones and strike columns run on over the frame and fade out on the table (the finishing pass had stopped the cones dead on the cloth's edge), and the ship's seam ends, faded out, at the cloth's edge.
- **Celebrations get their own time.** The sim holds the cleared cloth for 3.9 s after a perfect bonus wave and 2.8 s after a boss's death film (3.9 s after the last boss) and sends `celebrate` (`{ kind, secs, last, x, y, sprite }`: the length is `secs`, because `t` is every event's name). The results sheet waits for `renderer.celebrating()`. The fireworks are a designed show: eight single shells up the whole cloth (peony, ring, willow, crossette), then a six-shell finale. A boss gets a shorter show in its own colours from where it fell.
- **A light version of the celebration carries on with the results sheet up**: small shells placed above and below the sheet, and for eight seconds a few loose stitches falling on a small canvas above the page (`#fxtop`, body class `afterglow`), faint where they cross the sheet's text. With "remove animations" on there are no rockets, flashes or falling stitches: a few shells open slowly in place.
- **Paused means held.** The pause sheet used to wipe every moth, shot and the ship off the cloth behind it (the menu redraw ran); now the same frame is shown until play resumes. After a cleared stage the ship stays on the cloth under the results.
- **Every dive is announced.** A moth rears back for 0.3 s (0.12 before; 0.5 with Dive Warning, 1.0 with Tailor's Chalk as before) inside a danger rim that draws tight, with two chevrons marching the way it will go. Hawks start 3.6 units above the cloth instead of 1 and a chevron marks their column until they arrive. Strike columns fill steadily and a bright line races down them in the last third of the warning.
- **Hit-stop.** A heavy kill (a Spinner, a Weevil, anything that takes two hits) runs the whole game at 8% speed for 0.05 to 0.085 s, at most once every 0.3 s, never with "remove animations" on; inputs still land. Heavy moths burst with a dark thump, a thrown hoop of stitches and, for the heaviest, a second throw of their stitches and the lens leaning in 1%.
- **Each weapon has its own kick**: the ship recoils by weapon; only the Knot Bomb and the Wire Laser (and a little the Spread, Cross, Chain and Double) move the lens, because a lens that twitches at every shot of two always-firing weapons makes the weave shimmer.
- **Colour-blind-safe enemy shots** (`settings.cbShots`): every enemy shot becomes a bright cream hollow diamond on a wide dark disk, and dive warnings turn cream. Shape and brightness, not hue: the stock madder beads are dark red on dark blue.

## Polish pass: film kit (A4)

- **The Great Wick's entrance is a new film, "Strike", and it is longer**: 7.2 seconds of film, about 8 on the clock with its slow motion, where every entrance was 4. Why: the owner asked for the films to be remade as directed pieces; this one is the worked example for the other fifteen (`docs/FILM-KIT.md`). It is still skipped by a tap, and a boss seen before still gets the 1.5 second beat.
- **In that film the lanterns are brass cages hanging out from the cloth on swinging threads; in the fight they are the flat stitched loops they were.** The change-over happens under the white-out. Why: the film's camera goes low and close, where a flat loop is a line.
- **With "remove animations" on, a directed entrance is a still framed by the film's own camera** (cut to it, cut back), not the play camera with the film's props under it. Why: the still is the film's best frame, and a cut is not motion. The old films keep the old behaviour.
- **A film can use the whole phone screen.** The picture normally sits between the status row and the item row; both are hidden during a film, and a directed film may centre its picture on the screen and must be back before it ends.
- **Two films belong to no boss** (`ST.Cine.play('title')`, `play('ending')`): stand-ins until they are written. While one plays, the menu sheet is hidden and a tap or key skips it.
- **In "Strike" the Great Wick is first seen as a black shape.** In the wide he hangs unlit against the pools his three lanterns throw, his shadows swinging large on the cloth behind; only his eyes and then his coal light. Out of the white-out he is a black shape against a white-hot sheet that burns away to the fight. Why: lit from below in the dark his sparse chart read as a brown trellis, not a creature; the silhouette was also the best frame of the old film.
- **During that film the frame's top rail is flagged off in black.** Why: from the low camera the lit rail sat right behind the lanterns and read as a shelf. The fight is unchanged.
- **Boss warnings pulse instead of blinking, and burn steady at the end.** The basting used to blink on and off ever faster, up to 31 times a second; now it pulses smoothly 3 to 8 times a second and, in the last quarter of the warning, stops pulsing and burns steady and hotter (about to land). A front fills each warning as it runs (along a lane, across a row, out through a cone, a disc filling a circle inside a danger rim drawing tight), and lanes and cones carry chevrons marching the way the attack goes. Bright cream instead of red with colour-blind-safe shots on. Why: "clearer boss telegraphs", the same language as the moth dive warnings, and a large area flickering 31 times a second is not safe to look at.

## Polish pass: performance (B2)

- **Each stage is its own fabric**, not one coverlet: Coverlet (plain weave, as before), Linen, Tartan, Velvet, Knit, Felt, Silk damask, and a Royal weave (herringbone with gold lozenges) for the Moth King's stage, each with its own lamp, sky, light behind the cloth, frame wood, lacing and table (`C.PANELS[i].fabric`). Why: the plan's section 6 ("each of the eight stages on its own fabric") and the owner asked for it. All stay dark so every moth and enemy shot reads; the tartan's overcheck is a warm grey, not red, because a red dashed line already means danger. The same seven boro patches are laid differently on each.
- **On phones, shadows are stitched shadows**: each chart's own stitches flattened onto the cloth along the lamp into a soft half-size mask, and the frame's shadow baked into the cloth; desktop High keeps the real shadow map. Why: the shadow map was a second pass over everything plus a shadow lookup in every pixel of cloth, frame and table, the largest fixed cost on the owner's Pixel 7. Matched to the shadow map's darkness and shape by side-by-side captures at 412x915; the moth shadows are a touch softer.
- **On phones the glow is worked out at an eighth of the screen's size** (a quarter before), downsampled with sixteen taps and blurred with half steps, so its look is unchanged for about a quarter of the work. Phone High keeps its 2x MSAA: without it the cross-stitch charts read lacier; the frame test measures High without it.
- **The cloth is baked** when a stage starts (weave, patches, star-holes, the frame's shadow); only ripples, tears, the twinkle, the drifting light behind and heat are worked out per frame. The light behind the cloth now comes from a noise that repeats every 64 units (it drifts across a full repeat in about half an hour), so its pattern is a different draw of the same noise.
- **Menus and the hideout show the home cloth** (the Coverlet); a stage shows its own fabric from its first frame.
- **"Auto" graphics starts where this phone's frame test put it** (first launch, or Settings > Test this phone), not always on Light; it still steps down on its own.
- **A frame a hair short of 1/60 s still takes a game step** (`main.js`, up to 2.5 ms of slack). Why: frame times jitter around the screen's period, and the old loop gave a 60 Hz screen an occasional frame with no step followed by one with two, a visible hitch.

## Polish pass: interface (A2)

- **The hideout is one frame for every tab.** Header, tabs, a strip, the list, the details and the footer are rows of fixed size (tokens in `style.css`), so on a 412x915 phone the details panel starts at 525 px and is 300 px tall on every tab (it was anywhere from 620 to 741, 80 to 201 tall), the buy button sits at 764 on every tab and item (762 when there was one; three views had none), and the strip is 48 tall everywhere (41 to 56). Content changes; the frame does not. `tools/audit.py` fails if any named anchor moves on any tab, for any item, at any size, with normal or large text. Why: the owner saw things move and shift as tabs changed.
- **Text is fitted to its slot, not allowed to push.** A slot that is too small for its words steps its type down the type ramp (never under 12 px) and, at the last step, drops an optional line (`fit()` in `src/ui.js`, the `fit-1` to `fit-4` classes). The list always shows ten places, the empty ones ruled in.
- **The details panel has the same slots for everything**: picture, name and kind, what it does and what changes, then what you have on the left and the buy button on the right. When you cannot buy, the button stays where it is, muted, and says what is missing ("Need 11 more gems", "Needs its blueprint"); when there is nothing to buy, a stitched stamp takes its place ("Max level", "Flying"). Locked things are set back on the bare sheet with a greyed picture instead of being faded, so they can still be read.
- **The strip under the tabs always shows your ship's four numbers, each with what the selected thing would make it** (green up, red down). On Perks and Collection the same slot holds a two-way switch: Perks / Power-ups as before, and now Blueprints / Enemies, so the collection's two halves no longer share one crowded grid.
- **One documented set of design tokens** (`css/01-tokens.css`): the Mendnight colours plus text colours that each clear APCA Lc 75 on their ground, a 12 px floor type ramp, a 4-point spacing scale, layers, durations, the screen's safe-area insets and the drawn patterns. Every other line of `style.css` uses them. `style.css` is now built from the parts in `css/` by `node tools/css-build.js`; edit the parts.
- **The game's typefaces ship with it** (`fonts/`, OFL). Nothing is fetched from the network: the Google Fonts link and the two Three.js CDN fall-backs are gone.
- **The boss's name and health, and the bonus-wave count, float on the cloth** as lettering and a line of thread with a soft local shadow, not a solid band. Why: effects flaring up from a boss ended on the band's straight edge.
- **The lesson prompt has no box**: two lines on the cloth with a soft shadow and a short gold thread under them. It gives way to a banner and, on a phone, to a notice, and waits while any sheet is up. On a phone held sideways it sits in the side column, off the cloth.
- **Results sheets rise and are stitched on line by line, each number counting up**; a touch finishes it at once. While fireworks or a death film are still playing the sheet waits out of sight with no scrim. Behind a sheet the scrim is a vignette that fades to clear at the edges (about half strength at the heart during the afterglow), never a flat dim. On an upright phone the status row and item row step back while a results sheet, game over or victory is up, so nothing peeks round it.
- **Game over is the cloth itself, framed, with the thread that held the ship snapped across the top**, and a run's victory shows the eight bosses crossed off in red thread, the run's numbers and three lines of credits: design and direction Rob Lee, built with Claude, music and sound made with ElevenLabs.
- **The hideout has quiet ambient motion** behind its frame: lamplight that wavers, lint drifting down, and a red thread being drawn along the header's running stitch. CSS only, transform and opacity only, and none of it with "remove animations" on.
- **Settings gained Text size (Normal or Large, +15% on every reading size) and Colour-blind-safe shots** under Display, and "Test this phone" when `src/perf.js` is in the build. On a phone held sideways the Settings name, its three tabs and Back share one row so six settings fit without scrolling. Large text passes the same screen audit as normal text.
- **The title screen plays the title film once per launch** (`ST.Cine.play('title')`) with the page hidden under it; a tap skips it and the page fades in. Without a film, or with "remove animations" on, the page is simply there.
- **Android's back button closes the topmost thing** (`ui.back()`): "Test this phone", a sheet, a second view (Power-ups, Enemies), the route map back to the hideout, the hideout, records, how-to and save slots back to the screen before; at the title or main menu it does nothing, so Android can put the app in the background.
- **Every screen and the flight HUD keep clear of a cutout and the gesture bar** (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`), and the audit has a pass that declares both and fails anything a player reads or presses that sits under them.


## Boss films: Mother Tinea

- **Her entrance is a new film, "The Seam", 7.6 seconds of film (about 8.8 on the clock with its slow motion).** It is set inside the cloth at macro scale: a woven set where each thread is as thick as a rope, a seam of madder thread, three of her eggs, and her head pushing through. That head (a pelt of fine hairs and two faceted madder eyes) is a model made for the film, not her cross-stitch chart. Why: the approved idea for her is "macro, inside the weave", and at that scale a chart reads as a grid of crosses, not a creature. The cut to the fight happens under a white-out, onto her chart in its case where her script starts her.
- **At the end of that film, and when it is skipped, her silk case is already shut and full size.** Before, the fight grew the case in over about a third of a second after the entrance. Why: the handover should not show.
- **Her death is a new film, "Hours", 6.6 seconds: a day passing in time-lapse.** A window's light crosses the cloth, frost ferns spread and she is eaten away in branching runs, night falls and the cloth sags, a lamp comes on and one small moth rises into its cone of light. The window light is a painted layer (a stand-in until the art exists). Why: the approved idea for her death.

## Boss films: the Great Lappet

- **Her entrance is a new film, "Leaf Fall", 7.6 seconds of film (about 8.75 on the clock with its slow motion).** A shaft of low autumn sun slants into a dark room; one dead oak leaf tumbles out of the glare, past the lens and down the light, falls into focus on a patch of sun dappled with leaf shadows, lies still, stirs, and cracks open along its midrib into her wings; she rises into the shaft and the film cuts to the fight under the sun's bloom. The leaf is a model made for the film (an oak leaf in cross stitch, in her own chart's colours, hinged down its midrib, which is her pale stripe), not her chart. Why: the approved idea for her is "one dead leaf", and her chart folded reads as a crumpled moth, not a leaf.
- **Her death is a new film, "Gale", 6.4 seconds: a gale through the window strips her to a skeleton leaf.** Her colour drains to dead leaf, the wind takes the ochre cloth of her wings from the windward edge, then her veins one at a time, then her pale stripe, which is flung away; the wind drops, the light comes back to an empty cloth. Her chart is unpicked in an order made for this film (`lappetskel`, registered from `src/films/greatlappet.js`).
- **In both films the stage's cloth is taken almost to black** and lit only by the shaft and the patch of sun it lays on the cloth (a painted layer, a stand-in until the art exists). The fight's cloth and lighting are unchanged and are back by the end of each film.
- **During the low shots of her entrance the frame's top rail is flagged off in black**, as in "Strike". The fight is unchanged.

## Boss films: the Great Wick's death ("Drown")

- **The Great Wick's death is a new film, "Drown", and it is longer**: 6.9 seconds of film, about 8 on the clock with its slow motion, where the old death was 4. Why: the owner asked for every boss film to be remade as a directed piece; this is the partner of "Strike". A tap still skips it, and the fireworks still follow it at once.
- **In the film his stitched lamp becomes a real flame standing in a cup of wax.** Under the white of his last gasp the lamp's stitches go, and a flame stands in a pool of wax laid in satin-stitch rows; his body melts from the top down and runs into it in drips until the wax drowns the flame. Why: lit close, the stitched lamp and a real flame read as one white blob; the idea is a candle drowning in its own wax.
- **The house lights come back in three bands, top, middle and bottom, then the whole house**, each band lifting with a breaker's flicker and a brief amber glow before it is white. The lanterns are gone when the lights come up: they choke out in the film, and the game draws no lanterns once he is dead. Why: "the house lights come up one breaker at a time"; it ends on the game's own lit cloth with nothing of the film left.
- **With "remove animations" on, the death plays on the held play camera** (the kit's rule for deaths); the same story plays on the cloth without the flash, fringe or shake.

## Boss films: the Unraveller

- **Its entrance is a new film, "Loose End", 7.6 seconds of film (about 8.45 on the clock with its slow motion).** Everything before its mouth is filmed on a set made for the film: a knitted cloth at macro scale (each loop as big as a hand), standing in its own black world well clear of the game's cloth. A loose end of yarn at the cast-on edge is tugged twice from far away and rips free; the camera chases the running end up the knit, low and fast, while the rows behind it spring open into loops and drop into the dark; a reverse angle looks back at the hole racing after us; the camera whips up the yarn to its mouth (a model made for the film: rolled madder lips, a ring of steel needle points for teeth, a mist-green throat, the green-banded dome and brown bowl of its chart in knitting) and its steel hooks haul the yarn in hand over hand; the camera crashes down its throat. Out of the black it hangs where its script starts it, its jaws snap shut in a flash of mist, the last of the thread whips up into its mouth and its two hooks spring out to where the fight hangs them. Why: the approved idea for it ("one thread is pulled and the camera chases it up the cloth as the weave comes apart, into the mouth"); at the play scale the unravelling could not be seen, so the chase happens where a loop is big enough to spring open on screen.
- **The knit of that set is steel-coloured yarn warmed a little by its stage's own fabric (Knit)**, lit by one hard cold key raking across it; the boss's own mist green and madder appear only at its mouth. The fight's cloth is unchanged.

Each uses up several dive intervals, so total pressure stays near today's while it arrives in bursts. Weights grow by stage (`C.DIVES`).
- **Bonus rounds.** The figure for each stage is drawn from the deck (stage 1 is always the wave). Kinds: trace (today's), gold (18 of 40 moths are gold; a plain moth shot costs the combo and the perfect) and thread (24 gold threads fall along the figure's lanes; no shooting). The roster changes by stage (`C.SAMPLER.byStage`); bonus moths have at most 1 health so a hit or two still finishes them. `sampler.total` is therefore 40, 18 or 24, and `test/sim.test.js` was changed to accept those (its figure check now covers all 12).
- **Renderer.** Only small additions in `src/render.js`: a mark for each twist moth (`twistMark`), the burning thread along the top (`syncTimer`), a falling-thread drop shape, a gold-moth glow, an elite moth drawn 25% larger, and a faint glow on every moth in a dim cloth. `src/main.js`: the stage banner shows the wave's hint (its first twist, the boss preview or the bonus kind) in place of the patch name, and five new events play existing sounds.
- **Hawks and loopers are unchanged**, still on their timers; difficulty from patterns comes from formations, entries, dives and twists. A maxed bot now takes 0 to 2 hits in most late waves and up to 8 in a few (before: 0 to 4), a fresh ship still clears stage 1 in 20 to 40 s.
