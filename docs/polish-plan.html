1 · The cut-off effects: what I got wrong, and the real cause

Last time I fixed the canvas stopping short of the item row. That was a real cut, but not the one you see. I have now replayed a perfect bonus wave frame by frame and the cause is on the capture.

Cause A (the fireworks)
The fireworks need about 3 seconds: eight rockets, one every 0.24 s, each with a fuse and a burst. The game only waits 1.6 s after the last enemy before the results sheet comes up. The sheet and its dark scrim are a layer above the game, so the fireworks go off behind it. What you see is the strip of fireworks that pokes out above the sheet, ending on the sheet's straight top edge, and the game picture freezes under the sheet so the rest never plays.
Cause B (boss effects)
The same layering. The boss bar and the "Bonus wave" strip under the status row are solid bands drawn over the top of the cloth, exactly where a boss sits. Anything that flares upward from a boss stops on the straight bottom edge of that band. The results sheet does the same to the end of a boss fight.
Cause C (flat effects)
Shock rings, cloth ripples and thread trails lie flat on the cloth. The wooden frame stands above the cloth, so anything flat that spreads past the cloth edge disappears under a straight rail. Ripples are part of the cloth's own surface and simply end where the cloth ends.

The fix

  • Celebrations get their own time and their own layer. The results sheet waits for the fireworks (and for a boss's death film) to finish, then rises. While the sheet is up, the game keeps drawing behind it instead of freezing, and a light version of the fireworks continues over the sheet's edges on a top layer, so nothing ends on an edge.
  • The strip under the status row stops being a solid band. Boss health and the bonus counter are drawn as a floating thread and label with nothing behind them. Effects pass behind the text, not behind a wall.
  • Effects draw above the frame. Rings, sparks and trails render in a pass after the frame so they spill over the rails. Ripples that reach the cloth edge carry on as a soft ring across the frame and table.
  • Proof before the build. A new test plays a perfect bonus wave and every boss kill for real and saves the frames, and fails if any effect pixel row ends on a UI edge. You get the before and after sheets in the report.

Every tab sizes itself to its own content, so the frame moves under your thumb. The fix is one fixed skeleton shared by every tab: a fixed-height strip, a list area of fixed size, a details panel of one height with its parts in fixed slots (picture, name, before/after, price), and the buy button pinned to the same spot. Content changes; the frame never does. Long text shrinks to fit its slot or the slot's layout is redesigned, it never pushes.

  • I'll read your atelier repo first and audit the hideout, route map, results sheets, settings and title against it, then apply the same grid, spacing scale and type scale everywhere.
  • The screen audit gets a new rule: the named anchors (strip, list, details, buy button) must be at the same pixel position on every tab at every screen size, or the audit fails.
  • Tab changes animate the content only (a short cross-stitch wipe inside the list and details), never the frame.

3 · The double-shot sound

Two one-prompt attempts have missed, and I cannot hear them, so a third blind attempt is the wrong method. Instead:

  • Build it as a designed, layered sound: a tight low thump for weight, two bright string snaps a few milliseconds apart for the "double", and a short airy tail. Each layer is generated on its own and mixed, so each can be judged and swapped.
  • Small random pitch and timing changes per shot so it never machine-guns.
  • A Sound test page in Settings with three finished candidates for this weapon. You tap, pick, and the pick is what ships. The page stays in the game as a sound-effects jukebox, which players like anyway.

4 · Boss films, taken all the way

Today each film is a camera move over the game scene with particles. The next level is treating each as a short directed film with its own lens, light, score and art.

Score and soundA short musical sting per boss for the entrance and another for the death, plus 5 to 8 sounds per boss that exist nowhere else in the game, cut to the film's beats. Vibration is choreographed to the same beats.

Keeping the frame rate

The game is frozen during a film, so the film has the whole frame budget to itself. The lens effects scale by graphics tier, and each film must hold the frame-rate target in the benchmark below before it ships.

5 · Performance, proven instead of guessed

I have been tuning without a way to measure your phone. That changes first.

  • A benchmark built into the game (Settings → Graphics → Test this phone). It plays a fixed 40-second reel: a late-stage wave, a boss fight, a film, the fireworks. It records every frame time and shows the result per graphics tier. One tap copies the numbers so you can paste them to me. The same reel runs on first launch to pick the right tier for that phone.
  • Stutter from first use. New enemy types, effects and bosses compile their shaders and upload their art the first time they appear, which lands in the middle of a later stage. Everything is warmed up on the loading screen instead. This is a strong candidate for "worse as the stages go on".
  • Fewer, cheaper draws. Each enemy pose is its own draw, twice with shadows (52 draws in stage 1, 84 by stage 8). Merge all stitched sprites into one atlas and one draw; give phones soft blob shadows instead of a second full pass.
  • Cheaper pixels. The cloth is a procedural surface computed for every pixel every frame. Bake it to a texture when a stage starts and animate only what moves. Run the glow passes at half size.
  • 90 Hz. The Pixel 7 screen is 90 Hz and the game asks for 60. Decide with the benchmark whether to hold a clean 60 or offer 90 on High.
  • Budget rule for the build: the reel must hold the target with headroom on the tier the phone is given, or the build does not ship.

8 · How it gets built

  • Five workstreams that do not share files: effects layering and celebrations; menu skeleton and atelier audit; film toolkit and the sixteen films; performance and the benchmark; Play packaging and store assets. Sound runs alongside the films.
  • You said Fable subagents are fine for this, so I'll use them where the work is hardest (films, renderer) and say which did what.
  • Order inside the pass: benchmark and the cut-off fix first, because both tell us the truth about the current build. Films last, on top of the measured budget.
  • One build when every item is done, every test and audit passes, and the film contact sheets have been reviewed.
